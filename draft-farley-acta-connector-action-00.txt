           Connector-Action Profile for Acta Signed Receipts
                 draft-farley-acta-connector-action-00

Abstract

   Personal agent platforms let an agent act through connectors: a tool
   call reaches a service carrying a real credential, while the grant
   the person gave the agent stays inside the platform.  After the fact,
   the person, the platform, and the service each hold a different log,
   and none of them can show a third party what was authorized, what was
   received, and what happened.

   This document profiles Acta signed receipts for connector actions.  A
   platform presents, with each tool call, a small context naming the
   platform, the acting agent, and the grant.  The recorder of the
   receipt, a gate in front of the service or the service itself, writes
   that context into the receipt exactly as presented, records
   separately what it could verify about it, binds the digest of the
   call it actually received, and, when it observes the effect, the
   digest of what the service read back.  The receipt stays verifiable
   offline under the base specification, and a verifier holding the
   platform's key can test the platform's signature without trusting the
   recorder.

1.  Introduction

   An agent platform that runs personal agents (Meta's Muse, a coding
   agent such as Claude Code or Codex, any client of the Model Context
   Protocol) reaches services through connectors.  The platform decides
   what its agent may do: a grant for one action, a session, a task, a
   bounded time, or standing permission.  The connected service sees
   none of that.  It receives an ordinary call with an ordinary
   credential.

   Three consequences follow.  The service cannot tell a call the person
   granted from a call the agent invented.  Nobody can later prove to a
   third party which grant a call was made under.  And when the action
   is disputed, the person's log, the platform's log, and the service's
   log disagree about what was sent and what was done.

   The base specification [ACTA] defines signed receipts for tool
   decisions.  This document adds a profile for connector actions: a
   context object the platform presents with a call, and a member on the
   decision receipt in which the recorder writes that context, its own
   check of it, the digest of the call as received, and the observed
   effect.  The profile keeps presented facts and verified facts apart.
   A platform may claim any grant.  Only a signature under a key the
   recorder or the verifier trusts turns the claim into evidence, and
   the receipt says which it holds.

   The profile changes nothing about how a receipt is signed or
   verified.  A verifier that does not implement it ignores the member.
   A verifier that does implement it applies the rules in Section 4 in
   addition to those of the base specification.

   The key words "MUST", "MUST NOT", "REQUIRED", "SHALL", "SHALL NOT",

   platform  The system that runs the agent and holds the person's grant

   agent  The software acting for a person through the platform.

   grant  What the person allowed the agent to do, as the platform
      records it.

   context  The object the platform presents with a call: platform,
      agent, grant, and optionally the digest of the mandate the grant

   effect  What the service reports it did, read back after the action.

   verifier  Any party checking the receipt later, with or without keys
      beyond the recorder's.

2.  The Connector Context

   The context is a JSON object the platform builds once per call.  It
   is either presented bare or wrapped as an Acta envelope and signed by
   the platform.

2.1.  Fields

   {
     "type": "acta:connector-context",
     "platform": "meta.muse",
     "agent": "agent-e8d36428",
     "grant": {
       "type": "task",
       "id": "grant-42",
       "purpose": "Prepare the client review of pull request 25",
       "issued_at": "2026-09-20T05:44:36.339Z",
       "expires_at": "2026-09-21T05:44:36.339Z"
     },
     "mandate_digest": "sha256:aaaa...",
     "issued_at": "2026-09-20T05:45:00.000Z",
     "issuer_id": "sb:platform:vector-muse"
   }

   type (REQUIRED)  The string "acta:connector-context".

   platform (REQUIRED)  A stable name for the platform: lowercase
      letters, digits, dots and hyphens, at most 200 characters, such as
      "meta.muse" or "anthropic.claude-code".

   agent (REQUIRED)  The platform's identifier or public key for the
      acting agent, at most 200 characters.

   grant (REQUIRED)  An object.  Its member type (REQUIRED) is one of
      "one_time", "session", "task", "time_bounded", "perpetual".  Its
      members id and purpose are OPTIONAL strings, at most 200 and 500
      characters.  Its members issued_at and expires_at are OPTIONAL
      [RFC3339] timestamps in UTC; when both are present, expires_at
      MUST be later than issued_at.  The meaning of each grant type is
      the platform's; the profile records the type, it does not define
      the platform's permission model.

   mandate_digest (OPTIONAL)  The digest of the signed standard or
      mandate the grant sits under, in the form "sha256:" followed by 64
      lowercase hexadecimal characters, computed as a policy digest
      under Section 6.8 of [ACTA].

   issued_at (OPTIONAL)  When the platform built the context, as an



Farley                    Expires 24 March 2027                 [Page 4]

Internet-Draft        Acta Connector-Action Profile       September 2026


      [RFC3339] timestamp in UTC.

   issuer_id (REQUIRED when signed)  The platform's key identifier.  It
      MUST equal the kid of the signature that covers the context, so
      that the signer's identity sits inside the signed bytes.

   No other members are permitted.  String members MUST NOT contain
   control characters.  A context that fails any rule in this section is
   malformed.

2.2.  Signing

   A platform that can sign presents the context as an envelope: an
   object with the context as its payload member and a signature object
   with alg, kid and sig as its signature member.  The signing input is
   the JCS [RFC8785] canonicalization of the payload alone, and the
   signature is Ed25519 [RFC8032] over those UTF-8 bytes, exactly as
   Section 5.1 of [ACTA] specifies for a receipt.  A platform that
   cannot sign presents an envelope with the payload member only.

   The platform's key is distributed out of band.  A recorder or
   verifier MUST NOT take a key from the context, from the call, or from
   the receipt as the key to verify under; see Section 9.5 of [ACTA].

2.3.  Presentation over the Model Context Protocol

   Over MCP [MCP] the platform places the envelope in the request
   metadata of a tools/call request under the key "veritasacta.com/
   connector":

   {
     "jsonrpc": "2.0",
     "id": 7,
     "method": "tools/call",
     "params": {
       "name": "prepare_repository_review",
       "arguments": { "pull_number": 25 },
       "_meta": {
         "veritasacta.com/connector": {
           "payload": {
             "type": "acta:connector-context", "...": "..."

   Other transports carry the same envelope in whatever metadata channel
   they have.  The recorder reads the context from the call it received;
   it never reconstructs one.

3.  The connector Member on a Decision Receipt

   A recorder that received a context with a call adds a connector
   member to the payload of the decision receipt it issues for that
   call, as defined in Section 3.1 of [ACTA].  The member is covered by
   the receipt signature like every other payload member.

3.1.  Fields

















Farley                    Expires 24 March 2027                 [Page 6]

Internet-Draft        Acta Connector-Action Profile       September 2026


   "connector": {
     "v": 1,
     "context": { "type": "acta:connector-context", "...": "..." },
     "context_signature": {
       "alg": "EdDSA",
       "kid": "sb:platform:vector-muse",
       "sig": "..."
     },
     "context_check": "valid",
     "platform_kid": "sb:platform:vector-muse",
     "request_digest": "c7d2...",
     "effect": {
       "status": "confirmed",
       "readback_digest": "9f1a...",
       "observed_at": "2026-09-20T06:09:00.000Z"
     }
   }

   v (REQUIRED)  The integer 1.

   context (REQUIRED)  The presented context, verbatim, as defined in
      Section 2.1.

   context_signature (OPTIONAL)  The presented signature object,
      verbatim.  Present exactly when the platform presented one.

   context_check (REQUIRED)  What the recorder itself established:
      "unsigned" when no signature was presented; "unverifiable" when a
      signature was presented but the recorder held no key for its kid;
      "valid" when the recorder verified the signature under a key it
      holds for that kid; "invalid" when it held such a key and the
      signature did not verify.

   platform_kid (REQUIRED when context_check is "valid", otherwise
   absent)  The kid of the key the recorder verified under.  It MUST
      equal the kid of context_signature.

   request_digest (REQUIRED)  The SHA-256 digest, as 64 lowercase
      hexadecimal characters, of the JCS canonicalization of the object
      {"tool": name, "input": arguments} built from the call as the
      recorder received it, where input is an empty object when the call
      carried no arguments.  See Section 5.

   effect (OPTIONAL)  An object.  Its member status (REQUIRED) is one of







Farley                    Expires 24 March 2027                 [Page 7]

Internet-Draft        Acta Connector-Action Profile       September 2026


      "confirmed", "refused", "not_observed".  Its member
      readback_digest is the SHA-256 digest, as 64 lowercase hexadecimal
      characters, of the JCS canonicalization of what the service read
      back after acting; it is REQUIRED when status is "confirmed".  Its
      member observed_at is an OPTIONAL [RFC3339] timestamp in UTC.

   No other members are permitted.

3.2.  Recorder Rules

   *  The recorder MUST write the context and its signature exactly as
      presented.  It MUST NOT normalize, complete, or repair them.

   *  The recorder MUST set context_check from its own verification
      only.  A platform's statement about its own signature, or about
      the grant, MUST NOT influence the value.

   *  The recorder MUST NOT record a malformed context.  It MAY refuse
      the call, and it SHOULD log that a context was ignored, but the
      receipt it issues carries no connector member for a context the
      profile would reject.

   *  The recorder MUST compute request_digest from the call it
      received, never from a digest the platform presented.

   *  The recorder MUST add effect only after it has observed the
      effect, and MUST NOT set status to "confirmed" without a readback
      it computed the digest of.

   *  A recorder that issues one receipt per decision, as a gate does,
      attaches the member to the receipt of the decision for that call.
      A recorder that also observes the effect MAY issue a second
      receipt for the same call carrying the effect, chained to the
      first under Section 6.7 of [ACTA].

4.  Verification

   A verifier that implements this profile first verifies the receipt
   under Section 5.2 of [ACTA].  A receipt whose signature does not
   verify is invalid before the profile is consulted; a tampered
   connector member breaks the receipt signature like any other tampered
   member.

4.1.  Schema

   The verifier checks the member against Section 3.1 and Section 2.1,
   including the consistency rules: context_check "unsigned" only
   without a signature, any other value only with one; platform_kid
   present exactly when context_check is "valid" and equal to the
   signature kid; context.issuer_id equal to the signature kid whenever
   a signature is present; readback_digest present whenever
   effect.status is "confirmed".  A member that fails is a payload that
   does not satisfy the schema of its declared type in the sense of
   Section 5.3 of [ACTA], and the receipt is invalid.

4.2.  The Platform Signature

   When context_signature is present and the verifier holds a key for
   its kid obtained outside the receipt, the verifier MUST verify the
   signature over the JCS canonicalization of context under that key.
   If it does not verify, the receipt is invalid: the recorded context
   is not what the platform signed, whatever context_check says.  If it
   verifies, the verifier reports so; a recorded context_check of
   "invalid" or "unverifiable" then tells the verifier that the recorder
   held a different key, or none, for that kid, which the verifier
   SHOULD surface as a warning.

   When context_signature is present and the verifier holds no key for
   its kid, the platform signature is unverifiable.  This is not a
   failure.  The verifier MUST report it as such, MUST NOT report the
   receipt invalid on that account, and SHOULD say that the recorded
   context_check is the recorder's word alone.

   When context_signature is absent, nothing about the grant is
   established beyond the platform's presentation, and the verifier MUST
   say so.

4.3.  Outcomes

   The three outcomes of Section 5.3 of [ACTA] apply to the receipt as a
   whole.  Within the profile, the verifier MUST distinguish, in every
   form of output a consumer can act on: whether the member is well
   formed; the recorded context_check; and the verifier's own result for
   the platform signature, one of "absent", "unverifiable", "valid",
   "invalid".  The reference verifier names the two ways a receipt fails
   under the profile: connector_schema_invalid and
   connector_context_signature_invalid.

4.4.  What the Member Proves

   A valid receipt with a well-formed member proves that the recorder
   received a call whose digest is request_digest, that the platform
   presented exactly the recorded context with it, and, when effect is
   present, what the recorder observed the service report.  With a
   platform signature that verifies under a trusted key it also proves
   that the platform stood behind that context at the time.  It does not
   prove that the person understood the grant, that the grant type means
   what the verifier assumes, that the service did what it reported, or
   anything about calls that carried no context.

5.  Digest Construction

   Every digest in this profile is SHA-256 over the UTF-8 bytes of the
   JCS [RFC8785] canonicalization of a JSON value, encoded as 64
   lowercase hexadecimal characters. request_digest is computed over
   {"tool": name, "input": arguments}, with the arguments object as
   received and an empty object when there were none; member order in
   the source does not matter because JCS sorts it. readback_digest is
   computed over the JSON value the service read back, as the recorder
   holds it. mandate_digest carries the "sha256:" prefix because it is a
   policy digest under Section 6.8 of [ACTA]; the other two carry none
   because they are content digests in the style of action_ref in
   Section 2.2 of [ACTA].

6.  Test Vectors

   Seven vectors are published with the reference implementation at
   https://github.com/scopeblind/scopeblind-gateway in the file vectors/
   connector-action.v1.json.  Each is a complete decision receipt signed
   by a fixed gate key, with a connector member, the platform key the
   profile expects the verifier to be given, and the outcome required
   with and without that key.  Keys and timestamps are fixed, so the
   file is reproducible byte for byte from the source that builds it.

   unsigned-context  A bare context; recorded as unsigned; valid either
      way.

   signed-context-valid-confirmed  A signed context the recorder
      verified, with a confirmed effect and its readback digest; valid
      either way; with the key the platform signature is valid.

   signed-context-unverifiable-at-the-gate  A signed context the
      recorder held no key for; valid either way; with the key the
      verifier reports the signature valid and warns that the recorder
      could not check it.

Internet-Draft        Acta Connector-Action Profile       September 2026


   tampered-context-recorded-as-valid  The recorded context differs from
      what the platform signed, yet context_check says valid.  Invalid
      with the key (connector_context_signature_invalid); without the
      key the claim is unverifiable and the receipt stays valid with
      that warning.

   grant-type-outside-the-profile  grant.type is "forever"; invalid
      either way (connector_schema_invalid).

   confirmed-effect-without-readback  effect.status is "confirmed" with
      no readback_digest; invalid either way.

   unsigned-context-recorded-as-valid  No signature, yet context_check
      says valid and names a platform key; invalid either way.

   A conformant verifier reaches every listed outcome.  A verifier that
   reports the tampered vector valid while holding the platform key is
   taking the recorder's word for the platform signature, which this
   profile exists to make unnecessary.

7.  Security Considerations

   The security considerations of [ACTA] apply.  In addition:

   *  Presented is not verified.  The member's design keeps the
      platform's presentation, the recorder's check, and the verifier's
      own check as three separate facts.  Implementations MUST NOT
      collapse them, for instance by omitting context_check, or by
      treating a recorded "valid" as proof without holding the key.

   *  A platform that signs contexts attests that its agent acted under
      a grant it recorded.  It does not attest that the person read or
      understood the grant, and a verifier MUST NOT present the receipt
      as the person's consent to the specific call.  What the person
      agreed to is carried, when at all, by the mandate the
      mandate_digest names, under whatever rules govern that mandate.

   *  request_digest binds the call, not the moment.  Replay is
      addressed by the receipt's issued_at, by the chain of Section 6.7
      of [ACTA], and by whatever nonce or idempotency the service
      applies; the profile adds no replay protection of its own.

   *  grant.purpose is free text written by a platform or a person and
      may contain personal data.  Platforms SHOULD keep it short and
      specific to the action.  Verifiers do not need it to reach any
      outcome in Section 4, and receipts intended for wide distribution
      SHOULD carry it under the selective disclosure of Section 6 of
      [ACTA] where that is in use.



Farley                    Expires 24 March 2027                [Page 11]

Internet-Draft        Acta Connector-Action Profile       September 2026


   *  Platform keys are trust anchors and MUST be distributed as such:
      published by the platform at a stable location or given to the
      recorder and verifier through a channel the platform controls.  A
      recorder configured with a wrong key records "invalid" for honest
      contexts; a verifier with the right key will then report the
      discrepancy, which is the intended behaviour.

   *  A recorder that is the service itself attests its own effect.  A
      gate in front of the service that reads the effect back through
      the service's interface attests what the service told it.  Neither
      is an independent witness of the world; effect records what was
      read back, and the receipt says by whom.
